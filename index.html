The problem this exists to solve

James Fadiman has been thinking, speaking, writing, and teaching about psychedelics since 1961. He was a graduate student at Stanford when his former Harvard undergraduate adviser — Richard Alpert, who later became Ram Dass — introduced him to psilocybin in Paris. He guided Stewart Brand's first LSD trip in 1962. He participated in some of the last legal psychedelic research sessions before the government shut the field down. He was there at the beginning. He watched the entire field get buried. And he lived long enough to watch it come back.

The catalog includes Fadiman's 2011 and 2025 books and his 2019 paper with Sophia Korb. The paper describes an uncontrolled, self-selected reporting project involving more than 1,000 people in 59 countries. It does not establish efficacy, safety, or suitability for any person. Primary record: PubMed 30925850 ↗

He is 87 years old. He is doing two to three recorded interviews per month. He is still explaining the same foundational ideas to new audiences, because there is no other mechanism for those audiences to find what he has already said.

The oldest entry is Drugs in Our Culture, the 1968 KQED documentary he scripted and introduced. The newest is a 61-minute YouTube interview published April 25, 2026. That is 58 years of recorded thought on a single manually maintained page with no search, no topic index and no transcripts — and, as of this link check on 29 August 2026, 50 entries whose recording has been taken down and 8 more with no public URL at all.

Sources on this page were reviewed on 17 August 2026 and every link was re-checked on 29 August 2026. The public prototype helps people locate where historical topics appear in a catalog of recordings. It does not consolidate clinical answers, evaluate an individual's circumstances, or recommend a substance, dose, schedule, taper, interaction, or treatment. A catalog match is a discovery aid—not evidence that a statement is accurate.

What has been done — and what has been deliberately held

Current project status

The complete public archive has been catalogued entry by entry, and every link in it has been checked. The gap analysis has been run. The three books have been mapped as a separate evidentiary layer. The annotation schema has been fully designed and specified. The technical infrastructure — Vercel, Supabase pgvector, AssemblyAI, OpenAI embeddings, RAG query interface — is already live and running on other active projects.

The step that has not been taken is ingestion: downloading the audio, running transcription, putting his words into a system. That step has been deliberately withheld. The material is his. Beginning without his awareness felt like the wrong place to start something designed to honor sixty-plus years of work.

What the scope work covered: 132-entry archive catalogued; 10 verified gaps found; books mapped; 20+ academic papers inventoried; 12-field annotation schema specified; 8-layer technical architecture written with cost estimates; intellectual timeline 1961–2026 reconstructed.

The annotation schema — evidence-boundary specification

This proposed schema records what a source explicitly says. It must preserve the source date, speaker, quotation span, and review status. Public tooling does not translate historical terminology into dosing guidance, infer biology, or convert a topic mention into an indication.

FieldTypeControlled valuesKey logic
substanceenumLSD · psilocybin · MDMA · ibogaine · cannabis · ketamine · DMT · 5-MeO-DMT · mescaline · multiple · general_unspecifiedMatch compound or unambiguous synonym. Lower confidence when discussed via quoted third party.
historical_dose_languageverbatim text spansource wording only · unspecifiedPreserve only the language present in the source. The public Atlas does not map amounts to bands, schedules, or recommendations.
mechanism_languageverbatim text spanexplicit wording · none statedRecord explicit source language only. Do not infer a biological mechanism or scientific validity.
health_topic_mentioncontrolled topic + text spantopic named by source · noneA mention is not an application, indication, diagnosis, or treatment claim.

Default to human review when confidence <0.7.
claim_typeenumempirical_claim · recommendation · definition · historical_narrative · personal_anecdote · philosophical · hedge · opinionClassify the speech act, not the content.

Technical architecture — 8-layer specification

Layer 1 — Ingestion

YouTube/Vimeo (bulk, idempotent): yt-dlp -x --audio-format mp3 --audio-quality 0 --write-info-json --embed-metadata --download-archive archive.txt -o "%(upload_date)s_%(uploader)s_%(title).80s_%(id)s.%(ext)s" — --download-archive makes re-runs idempotent, critical for CI/CD. Podcast RSS: Python feedparser, deduplicate on guid. Books: EPUB → ebooklib + BeautifulSoup. Diarization: AssemblyAI speaker_labels=True as primary — bundles diarization + transcription + word-level timestamps in one API call.

Layer 2 — Transcription

Whisper large-v3 via faster-whisper (CTranslate2, ~4× speed) — required for period audio (1992 APA recording, 1997 transpersonal, Russian TV overdub, documented audio dropouts in the 2018 Toronto webinar). AssemblyAI API alternative: $0.17/hr (base + diarization) → ~$20–21 for full corpus. Modal A10G GPU for Whisper: ~$15–20. Speaker ID: longest-cumulative-talk-time rule + pyannote voiceprint reference from 3–4 known-Fadiman clips.

Layer 3 — Chunking

Hybrid semantic-within-speaker-turn. Target: ~400 tokens (300–500 band), 15% overlap. MinHash/SimHash deduplication for repeated stories (the "48 problems, 48 solutions" session appears across dozens of interviews). story_cluster_id links instances for evolution-layer comparison.

Layer 4 — Vector Database

OpenAI text-embedding-3-large (3072-dim, $0.13/1M tokens, <$5 one-time for full corpus). Supabase pgvector — keeps vectors, annotation metadata, and auth in one Postgres instance, enabling SQL WHERE filters combined with vector search. HNSW index: m=16, ef_construction=64.

Layer 5 — Annotation Pipeline

A future annotation pipeline may propose structured metadata, but no generated health or scientific interpretation may be auto-accepted. Source identity, date, quotation span, reviewer, limitations, and review status are required; health-related interpretation requires appropriately qualified independent review.

Layer 6 — Query Interface

Proposed retrieval: hybrid keyword and vector search with metadata filters, source IDs, timestamps, and an extractive-only result mode. Health-related queries return source passages and limitations—not generated answers. Topic maps may aid discovery but must expose source coverage, uncertainty, and review status. Rate limits, audit logs, and abuse monitoring are required before any inference endpoint ships.

Layer 7 — Evolution Layer

Every chunk carries a source date. "How has Jim's thinking on X changed from 2011 to 2025?" → filtered retrieval bucketed by year, structured timeline with inflection points and confidence trajectory. Topic emergence detection: first-occurrence by source_date per topic cluster. All driven by SQL aggregations over the same chunks table.

Layer 8 — Infrastructure & Deployment

Next.js on Vercel · Supabase pgvector · Upstash Redis (query caching TTL 24h, rate-limiting) · OpenAI · AssemblyAI · Modal (GPU transcription) · Cohere (reranking). Daily Vercel Cron re-scrapes watch-hear.html + runs yt-dlp --download-archive + checks RSS; new GUIDs auto-enqueue full pipeline.

What the cited research can and cannot show

Fadiman & Korb (2019). An uncontrolled, self-selected reporting project with more than 1,000 participants in 59 countries. It can describe reported experiences; it cannot determine efficacy, safety, causation, or individual suitability. PubMed 30925850 ↗

Bershad et al. (2019). A placebo-controlled low-dose LSD study in healthy volunteers with a narrow experimental scope. It is not treatment evidence and does not support personalized use. PubMed 31331617 ↗

Polito & Stevenson (2019). An observational study of self-reported microdosing. Observational associations do not establish therapeutic benefit or safety. PubMed 30726251 ↗

Scope rule. Atlas records and search results are historical discovery metadata. Health-related statements remain attributable to their original speakers and dates; they are not endorsed or converted into advice here.

Design principle — provenance before interpretation

Every statement the finished system produces should stay linked to its original recording, speaker, date, quotation span, and review status. The public prototype deliberately limits itself to catalog discovery and deterministic language flags.

Review principle — domain experts remain accountable

Health, scientific, legal, and safety claims require review by appropriately qualified independent professionals. The system must surface uncertainty and disagreement instead of generating a clinical conclusion.

Safety and limitations

This is an archival discovery tool, not medical guidance. It is not a dose calculator, contraindication screen, interaction checker, diagnosis tool, treatment tool, or emergency resource. It does not personalize output and must not be used to decide whether, when, or how to take, stop, combine, or change any substance or medication.

Catalog records and heuristic flags may be incomplete, outdated, or wrong. Follow source links, check publication dates, and seek appropriately licensed professional care for health decisions. In an emergency, contact local emergency services.

Where things stand and what comes next

This public build is a catalog, filterable explorer, timeline, deterministic term flagger, and local source-ID search prototype. It does not contain full transcripts, a production ingestion pipeline, verified semantic search, or a clinical knowledge system. The larger ingestion and review system remains unbuilt.

The step that has not been taken is ingestion: downloading the audio, running transcription, putting his words into the system. That step has been deliberately withheld. The material is his. Beginning without his awareness felt like the wrong place to start something designed to honor sixty-plus years of work.

To take this further — with his input on what he most wants it to do — the build is ready to begin. If the timing is not right, the scope work and infrastructure exist either way.
