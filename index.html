The problem this exists to solve

James Fadiman has been thinking, speaking, writing, and teaching about psychedelics since 1961. He was a graduate student at Stanford when his former Harvard undergraduate adviser — Richard Alpert, who later became Ram Dass — introduced him to psilocybin in Paris. He guided Stewart Brand's first LSD trip in 1962. He participated in some of the last legal psychedelic research sessions before the government shut the field down. He was there at the beginning. He watched the entire field get buried. And he lived long enough to watch it come back.

His 2011 book The Psychedelic Explorer's Guide mentioned microdosing almost as a footnote. That footnote became a global movement. His 2025 book Microdosing for Health, Healing, and Enhanced Performance is the first attempt to synthesize everything that movement has produced — self-reports from thousands of participants across 51 countries.

He is 87 years old. He is doing two to three recorded interviews per month. He is still explaining the same foundational ideas to new audiences, because there is no other mechanism for those audiences to find what he has already said.

The oldest entry in his archive is a 31-minute audio recording from the APA Centennial Conference, August 1992. The newest is a 61-minute YouTube interview published April 25, 2026. That is 34 years of recorded thought on a single manually maintained page with no search, no topic index, no transcripts, and 17 dead or missing URLs.

A researcher who wants to know what Fadiman has said about microdosing and antidepressant tapering has to either read all three books or listen to hundreds of hours of audio by hand. A clinician cannot find a consolidated, cited answer. A founder building a microdosing product cannot find in thirty seconds what the father of the protocol has actually said about their specific use case across a decade of interviews.

What has been done — and what has been deliberately held

Current project status

The complete public archive has been catalogued entry by entry. The gap analysis has been run. The full corpus has been mapped. The three books have been mapped as a separate evidentiary layer. The annotation schema has been fully designed and specified. The technical infrastructure — Vercel, Supabase pgvector, AssemblyAI, OpenAI embeddings, RAG query interface — is already live and running on other active projects.

The step that has not been taken is ingestion: downloading the audio, running transcription, putting his words into a system. That step has been deliberately withheld. The material is his. Beginning without his awareness felt like the wrong place to start something designed to honor sixty-plus years of work.

What the scope work covered: 132-entry archive catalogued; 10 verified gaps found; books mapped; 20+ academic papers inventoried; 12-field annotation schema specified; 8-layer technical architecture written with cost estimates; intellectual timeline 1961–2026 reconstructed.

The annotation schema — 12-field production specification

Applied to every text chunk (~300–500 words) from the corpus. Every interpretive field carries a confidence score (0.0–1.0). The mechanism claim and evidence quality fields require neuroscience training — they cannot be assigned accurately by pattern-matching on vocabulary.

FieldTypeControlled valuesKey logic
substanceenumLSD · psilocybin · MDMA · ibogaine · cannabis · ketamine · DMT · 5-MeO-DMT · mescaline · multiple · general_unspecifiedMatch compound or unambiguous synonym. Lower confidence when discussed via quoted third party.
dose_categoryenum (Fadiman's own framework)microdose (~10µg LSD) · museum_concert (~50µg) · creative (~100µg) · psychotherapeutic (~200µg) · transcendental (~400µg+) · unspecifiedMap stated µg/grams to bands, or descriptive language. Ambiguous "low dose" → microdose only if sub-perceptual is implied.
mechanism_claimenum + free-text span5HT2A · neuroplasticity · DMN · anti-inflammatory · mitochondrial · information_flow · placebo · none_stated · otherRequires neuroscience training. "Resets stress response" → HPA axis modulation, not neuroplasticity. Fadiman frequently disclaims mechanism ("we have no idea how it works") → none_stated with disclaimer captured.
condition_applicationmulti-enumdepression · anxiety · ADHD · chronic_pain · PMS · cluster_headache · PTSD · addiction · long_COVID · autism · bipolar · end_of_life · creativity · athletic · general_wellbeing · noneTag each named condition. Cap at distinct items in list-heavy chunks.
evidence_qualityenumpeer_reviewed_study · fadiman_citizen_science · anecdote · personal_experience · expert_opinion · speculation · historical_accountMost important field for scientific credibility. fadiman_citizen_science is distinct from generic anecdote. Default to human review when confidence <0.7.
claim_typeenumempirical_claim · recommendation · definition · historical_narrative · personal_anecdote · philosophical · hedge · opinionClassify the speech act, not the content.
confidence_flagenum (speaker certainty)high_certainty · moderate · low_speculative · explicit_uncertainty · disclaimedDistinct from evidence_quality — this is Fadiman's expressed certainty. Lexical hedging detection. Critical for trajectory visualization.
temporal_markerenumearly (1961–1979) · middle (1980–2009) · late (2010–2026)Derived primarily from source date. When recounting early events in a late interview, tag both recording_era and referenced_era.
audience_typeenumgeneral_public · researchers · clinicians · practitioners · founders · students · policy_legalInfer from host/venue metadata then confirm from register of language.
protocol_specificityenumspecific_protocol · general_observation · comparativeConcrete numbers/schedules → specific_protocol. Comparative: Fadiman vs. Stamets vs. intuitive.
citation_presentboolean + spantrue · falseTRUE when a named study, author, journal, or dataset is referenced. "Studies show" → FALSE.
contradiction_flagenum + linked IDsnone · intra-corpus_tension · evolution · self-correctionComputed in second pass via nearest-neighbor embedding. Always human-reviewed. Genuine evolution tagged evolution, not contradiction.

Technical architecture — 8-layer specification

Layer 1 — Ingestion

YouTube/Vimeo (bulk, idempotent): yt-dlp -x --audio-format mp3 --audio-quality 0 --write-info-json --embed-metadata --download-archive archive.txt -o "%(upload_date)s_%(uploader)s_%(title).80s_%(id)s.%(ext)s" — --download-archive makes re-runs idempotent, critical for CI/CD. Podcast RSS: Python feedparser, deduplicate on guid. Books: EPUB → ebooklib + BeautifulSoup. Diarization: AssemblyAI speaker_labels=True as primary — bundles diarization + transcription + word-level timestamps in one API call.

Layer 2 — Transcription

Whisper large-v3 via faster-whisper (CTranslate2, ~4× speed) — required for period audio (1992 APA recording, 1997 transpersonal, Russian TV overdub, documented audio dropouts in the 2018 Toronto webinar). AssemblyAI API alternative: $0.17/hr (base + diarization) → ~$20–21 for full corpus. Modal A10G GPU for Whisper: ~$15–20. Speaker ID: longest-cumulative-talk-time rule + pyannote voiceprint reference from 3–4 known-Fadiman clips.

Layer 3 — Chunking

Hybrid semantic-within-speaker-turn. Target: ~400 tokens (300–500 band), 15% overlap. MinHash/SimHash deduplication for repeated stories (the "48 problems, 48 solutions" session appears across dozens of interviews). story_cluster_id links instances for evolution-layer comparison.

Layer 4 — Vector Database

OpenAI text-embedding-3-large (3072-dim, $0.13/1M tokens, <$5 one-time for full corpus). Supabase pgvector — keeps vectors, annotation metadata, and auth in one Postgres instance, enabling SQL WHERE filters combined with vector search. HNSW index: m=16, ef_construction=64.

Layer 5 — Annotation Pipeline

Claude structured output enforcing JSON schema. Auto-accept at ≥0.80 confidence on all fields. Human review (RN Collins) for mechanism_claim, evidence_quality, and contradiction_flag at any confidence. Corrections feed as few-shot exemplars into prompt refinement.

Layer 6 — Query Interface

Semantic search: Next.js App Router → embed → pgvector ANN + metadata filters. Topic map: UMAP 2-D projection + HDBSCAN clustering → deck.gl. Ask Jim: top-20 retrieval → Cohere reranking to top 6–8 → answer prompt requiring inline citations to source_id+timestamp, returning "Fadiman doesn't appear to address this" when retrieval confidence is weak. Streaming via Vercel AI SDK. Upstash rate-limiting on inference endpoint.

Layer 7 — Evolution Layer

Every chunk carries a source date. "How has Jim's thinking on X changed from 2011 to 2025?" → filtered retrieval bucketed by year, structured timeline with inflection points and confidence trajectory. Topic emergence detection: first-occurrence by source_date per topic cluster. All driven by SQL aggregations over the same chunks table.

Layer 8 — Infrastructure & Deployment

Next.js on Vercel · Supabase pgvector · Upstash Redis (query caching TTL 24h, rate-limiting) · OpenAI · AssemblyAI · Modal (GPU transcription) · Cohere (reranking). Daily Vercel Cron re-scrapes watch-hear.html + runs yt-dlp --download-archive + checks RSS; new GUIDs auto-enqueue full pipeline.
